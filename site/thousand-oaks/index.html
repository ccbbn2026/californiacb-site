Local knowledge

Building in Thousand Oaks: what to plan for.

Oak tree protection

Thousand Oaks protects every oak tree over two inches in diameter. The protected zone extends five feet beyond the canopy or 15 feet from the trunk, whichever is greater, and building or grading inside it requires an oak tree permit. An oak near your planned addition can change where the addition goes, so we check for one first.

Hillside lots and fire zones

Much of the city’s hillside edge is mapped in fire hazard severity zones, and the 2018 Woolsey Fire burned into parts of the area. New construction in those zones must meet California’s wildland-urban interface standards for ignition-resistant materials, vents, eaves and windows.

HOA review

Many Thousand Oaks neighborhoods have HOAs that review exterior changes. We match rooflines, roofing and stucco to the existing house, as we did on our Oxnard addition, and plan the HOA submittal alongside the city permit.

Fire review

Thousand Oaks is served by the Ventura County Fire Protection District, which reviews fire requirements such as sprinklers, access and hydrant distance. On hillside lots, fire access can shape the site plan as much as the building does.

Questions

Thousand Oaks project FAQ

Do I need an oak tree permit for my addition?

If any building or grading falls within an oak’s protected zone, five feet beyond the canopy or 15 feet from the trunk, whichever is greater, the city requires an oak tree permit. We identify protected trees before design so the addition can work around them.

Can I build an ADU if my home is in an HOA?

In most cases, yes. California law limits an HOA’s ability to prohibit or unreasonably restrict ADUs, though the association can still review the design.

Do I need to replace parking if I convert my garage to an ADU?

Generally no. California law does not require replacement parking when a garage is converted into an ADU. We confirm the details for your property during planning.

Is California Custom Builders licensed?

Yes. California Custom Builders holds California contractor license #929421 and is licensed, bonded and insured. You can confirm the license on the Contractors State License Board website.
