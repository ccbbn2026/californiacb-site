Local knowledge

Building in Santa Paula: what to plan for.

Historic properties

Santa Paula has a Historic District overlay. If a property is a designated landmark or sits within a historic district, alterations, additions and demolition need an alteration permit and a hearing before the city’s Historic Preservation Commission. We check a property’s status first, because it affects both the design and the schedule.

Older homes, hidden conditions

Older houses often have raised foundations, aging electrical service and plumbing that has been patched over the decades. We look at these before pricing, and we match new work to old the way we did on our Ventura addition: foundation height, windows, stucco and interior details.

Fire country

The 2017 Thomas Fire started just outside Santa Paula, and hillside properties around the city fall within fire hazard severity zones that require ignition-resistant construction. We rebuilt a home in nearby Ojai after the same fire; see the Ojai fire rebuild case study.

Fire review

Fire plan review in Santa Paula is handled by the Ventura County Fire Protection District, which covers requirements such as sprinklers, access and hydrant distance. We plan for those reviews alongside the city’s building permit.

Questions

Santa Paula project FAQ

Do historic homes in Santa Paula need special approval?

If the property is a designated landmark or is within a historic district, changes require an alteration permit and a Historic Preservation Commission hearing. Many older homes are not designated, so we confirm the status before design begins.

Can I add an ADU to an older Santa Paula property?

Usually, yes. The deciding factors are the lot, the existing utilities and, for designated historic properties, the design review. We look at the sewer, electrical service and water line early, since older homes often need upgrades.

Do you rebuild homes after a fire?

Yes. We rebuilt a home in Ojai after the Thomas Fire, growing it from 1,600 to 2,400 square feet with a new three-car garage in five months of construction.

Is California Custom Builders licensed?

Yes. California Custom Builders holds California contractor license #929421 and is licensed, bonded and insured. You can confirm the license on the Contractors State License Board website.
