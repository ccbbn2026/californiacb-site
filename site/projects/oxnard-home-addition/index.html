Case study · Oxnard · 2020

Home addition and kitchen expansion.

A new primary suite and a larger kitchen and living room, finished in time for Thanksgiving.

The project

The owners wanted a primary bedroom and bathroom of their own, plus more room where the family spends its time: a bigger living room and kitchen. That meant opening up the existing house rather than building a separate wing, so the new space had to look and feel like it had always been there.

The challenges

  • Opening the existing house. Existing exterior walls had to be demolished to connect the addition to the living room and kitchen, while the rest of the home stayed intact.
  • A new foundation tied to the old house. The addition needed its own new foundation, built to join the existing structure.
  • Making the addition disappear. The new roof had to match the existing roof, and the stucco texture had to match the original, so the addition wouldn't stand out from the street.
  • Working within the existing utilities. The larger home had to be served by the same utility sizes the house already had.

How we handled it

One house, not a house with an addition.

Structure

We handled every part of the construction: demolishing the exterior walls, pouring the new foundation, and framing the addition into the existing house.

Exterior match

We built the new roof to match the existing one and matched the stucco texture to the original. While the walls were open, we replaced all of the home's old windows with new-construction windows.

Interior finishes

Maple cabinets, quartz countertops, tile flooring, crown molding and new double doors into the primary bedroom tie the new and existing rooms together.
